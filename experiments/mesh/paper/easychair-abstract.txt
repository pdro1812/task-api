Title:
Sidecars, Ambient, or eBPF? Measuring the Tail-Latency and Resource Cost of Kubernetes Service Meshes

Keywords:
service mesh; Kubernetes; tail latency; performance evaluation; eBPF; energy efficiency

Abstract:
Service meshes add encryption, telemetry, and traffic policy to microservices, but each data-plane design pays for them differently: a proxy per pod (sidecar), a per-node L4 proxy with optional shared L7 waypoints (ambient), or in-kernel eBPF with a per-node proxy. We measure seven configurations (no mesh, Istio sidecar, Istio ambient with and without waypoints, Linkerd, and Cilium with WireGuard, with and without L7 policy) on a pinned, virtualized Kubernetes testbed with open-loop load, using an echo service, Online Boutique, and DeathStarBench Hotel Reservation. On Online Boutique at the middle rate, the Istio sidecar adds 52 ms to p99 latency and ambient L4 6.7 ms, and the meshes keep 2.0 (Cilium without L7) to 9.1 (Cilium with L7) more cores busy per 1000 req/s than no mesh; energy per request grows by 16 to 188% at the highest rate. Among meshes that authenticate workloads, ambient L4 has the lowest latency, CPU, and memory and the highest throughput; Cilium without L7 policy is cheaper on two of the three applications but authenticates only nodes. Profiling attributes most of the overhead to HTTP/2 processing for the Istio sidecar, TLS for ambient L4, and, for Cilium, the redirect to its per-node Envoy and Hubble flow logging, not its eBPF programs. A compositional model predicts the held-out application's p99 overhead within 11-13% for L4 data planes, and a bottleneck law predicts saturation throughput within 20% on the two microservice applications; for L7 data planes, neither per-hop costs nor node utilization predict the tail, and relieving the busiest node did not reduce it.
