Title:
Sidecars, Ambient, or eBPF? Measuring the Tail-Latency and Resource Cost of Kubernetes Service Meshes

Keywords:
service mesh; Kubernetes; tail latency; performance evaluation; eBPF; energy efficiency

Abstract:
Service-mesh data planes differ in where they process traffic: a proxy per pod (sidecar), a per-node L4 proxy with optional shared L7 waypoints (ambient), or eBPF with a per-node proxy. We measure seven configurations (no mesh, Istio sidecar, Istio ambient with and without waypoints, Linkerd, and Cilium with WireGuard, with and without L7 policy) on a pinned, virtualized Kubernetes testbed with open-loop load and three applications. On Online Boutique at the middle rate, the Istio sidecar adds 52 ms to p99 latency and ambient L4 6.7 ms; the meshes keep 2.0 (Cilium without L7) to 9.1 (Cilium with L7) more cores busy per 1,000 req/s, and up to 188% more energy per request. Among meshes that authenticate workloads, ambient L4 has the lowest latency, CPU, and memory and the highest or near-highest throughput; Cilium without L7 policy, measured on a later day, is as cheap or cheaper on the two microservice applications but authenticates only nodes. Profiling attributes most of the overhead to HTTP/2 processing for the Istio sidecar, TLS for ambient L4, and, for Cilium, the redirect to its per-node Envoy and Hubble flow logging, not its eBPF programs. A per-hop model fitted on two applications explains the third within 11-13% for L4 data planes, and node utilization bounds throughput within 19% on the microservice applications. For L7 data planes, per-hop costs and node utilization do not explain the tail; the load on the busiest proxy instance does better for per-pod sidecars, and relieving the busiest node did not reduce the Istio sidecar's tail.
